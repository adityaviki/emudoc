What it does with what you upload

Ask, and follow the citation

The assistant reads each document's outline, searches its passages and looks up its facts before it answers. Each claim cites the passage it rests on, and a click opens the document there. Ask about the whole project, a folder or the documents you chose.

Find it by its words or its meaning

Search a project, or every project you see, by the words a passage uses and by what it means, whatever language it is written in. Narrow it by type, date or folder, and search again within what it found.

Keep the library in order

Folders, favourites and recent documents; each document's title, type and date beside what the reading found, its versions and its copies.

Take the answer with you

Download an answer or a whole chat as a Word document, a PDF or Markdown, its citations as numbered notes. Share a chat with the project, and export a project whole, its originals, outline, facts and chats.

Read the facts into a table

Dates, amounts, parties and lines, read into a table you sort and filter, each cell with the words it came from. Extraction templates say which fields to read from each kind of document, and a corrected value is kept.

Check one document against another

Cross-checks compare an invoice with its purchase order or a price with the price list, and say where they differ. Automations run them as documents arrive, and reviews keep who approved what.

Write from what you found

A dispute letter, a reply or a memo, drafted from the project's documents with its citations, edited in versions and downloaded.

Ask the same questions every week

Save a project's questions as a set, asked on a schedule or as documents arrive, each run a report of the answers.

Talk it over beside the passage

Comment on the words you chose in a document, mention a colleague, and read what was done in the project lately.

  • On the record. The audit log keeps who did what in the organisation, and a project or an account can be exported whole, or deleted.
