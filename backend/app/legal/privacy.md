# Privacy policy

Version of $published.

This policy says what personal data Emudoc keeps, why, for how long, who else receives it, and
what you can ask of us. $operator ("we", "us") runs this Emudoc service and is responsible for
it. Write to $contact about anything in it.

## Who decides about which data

We decide about the data of your account: who you are, how you sign in, and what the service
records to keep it secure. The documents in an organisation's projects, and the questions asked
and answers given about them, are the organisation's: it decides what goes in, and we process
them on its behalf, as section 6 of the [terms of service](/terms) describes. If an
organisation uploaded a document about you, ask the organisation first; we help it answer you.

## What we keep, and why

- **Your account**: your email address, your name, the time zone our email to you is dated in,
  how you want to be told each kind of notice, when you ended the tour of the app and hid its
  list of first steps, your password (as a hash only, from which it cannot be read back), whether you confirmed your
  address, the Google or Microsoft account you sign in with, and with two-factor sign-in your
  authenticator's secret (encrypted) and the hashes of your recovery codes, and which version
  of these terms you accepted and when. We need them to give you an account, under our
  contract with you.
- **Where you signed in**: for every browser or device you sign in from, its internet (IP)
  address, the browser's description of itself and when it was last used, all of which the
  account page shows you, and the address each sign-up, confirmation, reset or
  change-of-address link was sent to; and for every browser you signed in with, a random code it keeps in a cookie
  (as a hash only) and when it first and last signed in, so that it can still sign in while
  someone guessing your password has paused sign-ins from other browsers. We keep them to keep
  your account secure, which is our legitimate interest.
- **What you upload and ask**: the documents, what is read from them (their text, images of
  their pages, summaries, facts and the search index), and your chats with their questions and
  answers. We need them to provide the service.
- **Invitations**: the email address a member of an organisation invited to join it, the role
  offered and who sent it, to mail the invitation and let whoever holds the address join; the
  organisation's owners and admins see it until it is accepted or revoked. We need it to
  provide the service to the organisation.
- **Usage**: the pages, questions and model use your organisation counted, and what they cost,
  each with the member it was for, to hold the organisation to its plan and, on a paid plan,
  to bill it.
- **The audit log**: who signed in, uploaded, downloaded, deleted or shared what, and when,
  with the IP address of the request. Your organisation's owners and admins can read it, to see
  what happens to their documents, which is their and our legitimate interest. It never holds
  what a document, a question or an answer says.
- **Abuse protection**: how many requests each account, each IP address, each email address
  (as a hash only) and each browser you signed in with made in a minute, and how many wrong
  passwords and codes they were tried with in an hour, to slow down password guessing and
  floods.
- **Server logs**: the IP address, time and path of each request and the account and project it
  was for, and the service's own record of what it did, to keep it running and to find faults.
  They never hold what a document, a question or an answer says, nor an email address.

## How long it is kept

- **Documents**, and everything read from them, until a member deletes them or the
  organisation is deleted.
- **Chats**, for as long as the organisation's plan keeps a chat after its last question
  ($chat_retention), or the terms agreed with the organisation in its place, unless deleted
  sooner; the chats you did not share with a project go when you leave the organisation or an
  owner or admin removes you from it. After a move to a plan or terms that keep chats for less
  time, they are kept as before for $held_days days after the move.
- **Audit log entries**, for as long as the organisation's plan keeps them
  ($audit_retention), or the terms agreed with the organisation in its place; after a move to
  a plan or terms that keep them for less time, as before for $held_days days after the move.
- **Sessions and links**, until $credential_days days after they expired or were ended.
- **Invitations**, until they are accepted or revoked, or $credential_days days after they
  expired.
- **The browsers you signed in with**, until $browser_days days after their last sign-in.
- **The notices in your notification centre**, $notification_days days, read or not; those
  about an organisation go when you leave it or an owner or admin removes you from it.
- **Abuse protection counts**, at most $count_hours hours.
- **Server logs**, $server_logs
- **Usage records**, as long as the organisation exists.
- **Your account**, until you delete it. It goes $deletion_days days after you ask, with every
  organisation of which you are the only member; in an organisation with other members, the
  usage and audit log entries about you stay with the organisation without your name and your
  IP addresses.

## Cookies and storage in your browser

Emudoc sets three cookies, all needed for the service to work and its accounts to stay secure:
`$session_cookie`, which keeps you signed in for up to $session_days days; `$oauth_cookie`, which
lasts $oauth_minutes minutes while you sign in with Google or Microsoft; and `$browser_cookie`, a
random code that tells a browser you signed in with from others for $browser_days days after its
last sign-in, also once you have signed out, and goes with no request but those about signing in
and your account. Your browser's own storage keeps your choice of a
light or dark theme, which documents and folders you had open, how wide you made the panes,
which notices you dismissed, and whether it shows your notices as they come. There are no analytics, advertising or tracking cookies, and
$third_parties so there is nothing to consent to.

## Security

- Connections to the service are encrypted, and so are its connections to anything outside its
  own network.
- Passwords are kept only as hashes, and two-factor sign-in is available, its authenticators'
  secrets encrypted with a key the database does not hold. After many wrong passwords or
  codes, sign-ins from the browsers that have never signed in to the account pause for the
  rest of the hour, and the account's owner is told.
- Every request is checked against the organisation it is for, so no organisation can reach
  another's data.

## Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your personal data, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict or
object to how we use it, and to have it in a machine-readable form to take elsewhere. Three of
these you can do yourself on the account page: you can correct your name and email address
there, "Download your data" gives you a copy as a file, and "Delete account" deletes it. For
anything else, write to $contact. You can also complain to
the data protection authority where you live or work, or where we are established.

Emudoc is a tool for work and is not meant for children under 16.
