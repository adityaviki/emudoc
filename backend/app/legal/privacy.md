# Privacy policy

Version of $published.

This policy says what personal data Emudoc keeps, why, for how long, who else receives it, and
what you can ask of us. $operator ("we", "us") runs this Emudoc service and is responsible for
it. Write to $contact about anything in it.

## Who decides about which data

We decide about the data of your account: who you are, how you sign in, and what the service
records to keep it secure. The documents in an organisation's projects, and the questions asked
and answers given about them, are the organisation's: it decides what goes in, and we process
them on its behalf, as section 6 of the [terms of service](/terms) describes. If an
organisation uploaded a document about you, ask the organisation first; we help it answer you.

## What we keep, and why

- **Your account**: your email address, your name, the time zone our email to you is dated in,
  how you want to be told each kind of notice, when you ended the tour of the app and hid its
  list of first steps or its suggestion to invite your team, your password (as a hash only,
  from which it cannot be read back), whether you confirmed your address, the Google or Microsoft account you sign in with, and with two-factor sign-in your
  authenticator's secret (encrypted) and the hashes of your recovery codes, and which version
  of these terms you accepted and when. We need them to give you an account, under our
  contract with you.
- **Where you signed in**: for every browser or device you sign in from, its internet (IP)
  address, the browser's description of itself and when it was last used, all of which the
  account page shows you, and the address each sign-up, confirmation, reset or
  change-of-address link was sent to; and for every browser you signed in with, a random code it keeps in a cookie
  (as a hash only) and when it first and last signed in, so that it can still sign in while
  someone guessing your password has paused sign-ins from other browsers. We keep them to keep
  your account secure, which is our legitimate interest.
- **What you upload and ask**: the documents, with the files they had before a new version and the
  text read from those, what is read from them (their text, images of their pages, a PDF made of a
  Word, PowerPoint, Excel, RTF or HTML file to show it as it looks, summaries, facts
  and the search index), the facts you checked or corrected with when, and the documents you put in
  review, approved or disputed with when and those assigned to you, which your organisation's
  members see beside your name, your chats with their questions and answers, the chats you pinned to
  the top of your list, the documents you starred and the last $recent of each project's you opened
  with when, the titles, kinds and dates you gave documents in place of what was read from them,
  the notes on documents you wrote, which your organisation's members see beside your name until
  another changes them, the runs of a project's
  cross-checks you made, with the values they compared and the reports the model wrote from them,
  the drafts you asked the model for or edited, with what you asked of them and every version,
  the files you keep in your My files in a project, with every version, which only you see,
  the question sets you made, with their questions and when they are asked, and the reports
  of their answers, which your organisation's members who see the project read, and the comments
  you wrote on documents, with the words they quote and the members they mention, which those
  members read beside your name and which stay with the document, without your name, once your
  account is deleted.
  We need them to provide the service.
- **Invitations**: the email address a member of an organisation invited to join it, the role
  offered and who sent it, to mail the invitation and let whoever holds the address join; the
  organisation's owners and admins see it until it is accepted or revoked. We need it to
  provide the service to the organisation.
- **API keys**: for each key an organisation's owner or admin makes for its programs, its name,
  what it may do, who made it (it acts as them), when it was made, last used and runs out, and
  the key itself as a hash only; and when a program names a request with an idempotency key,
  that key and the answer the request was given, which holds what it made, so that the request
  sent again is given the same answer rather than carried out twice. We need them to provide
  the service to the organisation.
- **Webhooks**: for each webhook an organisation's owner or admin adds for its programs, its
  address, the events it is told of and who added it, its signing secret, encrypted, and for
  each event sent to it which it was, about which document or run, when, and how the address
  answered, never what the event said, so that the organisation's programs hear of its work as
  it happens. We need them to provide the service to the organisation.
- **Share links**: for each link a member makes that shows a document, or an answer with its
  question and the passages it cites, to whoever holds it, which document or answer, who made
  it, until when it runs, its password as a hash only if it has one, how
  often and when it was last opened, and the link itself as a hash only; and for each time it
  is opened, a random code its browser keeps in a cookie (as a hash only) and until when that
  lets it read the document. The organisation's audit log records each opening of the link, and
  each download of the document's file through it, with the IP address it came from: if a
  document or an answer was shared with you by a link, the organisation that shared it sees when,
  and from which address, you opened it, and nothing else of you. We need them to provide the service to
  the organisation.
- **Usage**: the pages, questions and model use your organisation counted, and what they cost,
  each with the member it was for, to show the organisation what it used and what that cost.
- **Messages from the help menu**: what you write from the app's help menu, with your name,
  email address and role, the ids of your account and organisation, the page you wrote from,
  the version of the service, your browser's description of itself and the reference of the
  last request the page saw fail, so that whoever answers can help you and find what went

- **The audit log**: who signed in, uploaded, downloaded, deleted or shared what, and when,

- **Abuse protection**: how many requests each account, each IP address, each email address

  address opened in a minute and how many wrong passwords each link and address were tried
  with in an hour, and how many messages each account sent from the help menu in an hour, to slow down
  password guessing and floods.
- **Server logs**: the IP address, time and path of each request and the account and project it

  They never hold what a document, a question or an answer says, nor an email address.

- **Authorities**, only where the law obliges us to hand data over.

## How long it is kept

- **Documents**, their earlier versions and everything read from them, until a member deletes
  them or the organisation is deleted with everything else it holds, its usage records and audit
  log among them.
- **Project exports**: the file of a project's documents and chats that a member asks for is
  kept $export_days days after it is made, for that member alone, and goes sooner with the
  project, or with the member's place in the organisation.
- **The trash**: a project, folder, document or chat a member deletes is kept in its trash for
  $trash_days days, where it can be restored, and is then deleted for good, unless a member
  deletes it there sooner; a chat goes sooner when the organisation keeps chats for less time.
- **Chats**, until they are deleted, or for as many days after their last question as the
  organisation's owners or admins chose; the chats you did not share with a project go when an
  owner or admin removes you from the organisation.
- **Runs of cross-checks**, with what they found and their reports, for as long as the
  organisation keeps a chat, counted from when the run was made, unless a member deletes them
  sooner.
- **Drafts**, with their versions, for as long as the organisation keeps a chat, counted from
  when one was last asked for or saved, unless a member deletes it sooner.
- **Your My files**, with their versions, until you delete them, you are removed from the
  organisation, your account is deleted or their project goes.
- **Question sets**, until a member deletes them or their project goes; **the reports of their
  answers**, for as long as the organisation keeps a chat, counted from when the report was
  made, unless a member deletes them sooner.
- **Audit log entries**, $audit_days days, or as long as the organisation's owners or admins
  chose instead.
- **Sessions and links**, until $credential_days days after they expired or were ended.
- **Invitations**, until they are accepted or revoked, or $credential_days days after they
  expired.
- **API keys**, until they are revoked or the member who made them is removed from the
  organisation, or $credential_days days after they ran out.
- **Share links**, until they are revoked or expire, at most $share_days days after they were
  made, or sooner when the member who made them is removed from the organisation; the code of
  each opening, $share_view_minutes minutes at most.
- **The answers kept for a program's idempotency keys**, a day, or until its API key goes.
- **Webhooks**, until an owner or admin removes them or the organisation goes; what was sent to
  each, $webhook_days days.
- **The browsers you signed in with**, until $browser_days days after their last sign-in.
- **The notices in your notification centre**, $notification_days days, read or not; those
  about an organisation go when an owner or admin removes you from it.
- **Abuse protection counts**, at most $count_hours hours.
- **Server logs**, $server_logs
- **Usage records**, as long as the organisation exists.
- **Your account**, until you delete it. It goes $deletion_days days after you ask, with every
  organisation of which you are the only member; in an organisation with other members, the
  usage and audit log entries about you stay with the organisation without your name and your
  IP addresses.
- **Backups**: $backups

## Cookies and storage in your browser

Emudoc sets five cookies, all needed for the service to work and its accounts to stay secure:
`$session_cookie`, which keeps you signed in for up to $session_days days, or fewer while you work
in an organisation that asks its members to sign in more often; `$oauth_cookie`, which
lasts $oauth_minutes minutes while you sign in with Google or Microsoft; `$connect_cookie`, which
lasts $connect_minutes minutes while you connect a folder of yours to a project; `$share_cookie`, which
lasts up to $share_view_minutes minutes while you read a document someone shared with you by a
link, and goes with no request but that link's; and `$browser_cookie`, a
random code that tells a browser you signed in with from others for $browser_days days after its
last sign-in, also once you have signed out, and goes with no request but those about signing in
and your account. Your browser's own storage keeps your choice of a
light or dark theme, which documents and folders you had open, how wide you made the panes,
which notices you dismissed, and whether it shows your notices as they come. There are no analytics, advertising or tracking cookies, and
$third_parties so there is nothing to consent to.

## Security

- Connections to the service are encrypted, and so are its connections to anything outside its
  own network.
- Passwords are kept only as hashes, and two-factor sign-in is available, its authenticators'
  secrets encrypted with a key the database does not hold; an organisation may require it of
  everyone who works in it. After many wrong passwords or
  codes, sign-ins from the browsers that have never signed in to the account pause for the
  rest of the hour, and the account's owner is told.
- Every request is checked against the organisation it is for, so no organisation can reach
  another's data.
- The people who run the service see no member's account or documents.

## Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your personal data, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict or
object to how we use it, and to have it in a machine-readable form to take elsewhere. Three of
these you can do yourself on the account page: you can correct your name and email address
there, "Download your data" gives you a copy as a file, and "Delete account" deletes it; the
documents of a project, with what was read from them and its chats, you export from the projects
page as a member of its organisation. For anything else, write to $contact. You can also complain to
the data protection authority where you live or work, or where we are established.

Emudoc is a tool for work and is not meant for children under 16.
