# Searching and asking

## Searching a project

**Search the project**, the box under the library's buttons, searches as you type. The files
whose names hold your words come first, under "Files", as rows of the library, each with the
folder it is in; the passages that hold them are listed below, under "Passages". Passages are
found by their words and by their meaning together, so "late delivery penalty" also finds a
clause about liquidated damages. Click a passage to open its document at that passage beside the
library, the search staying where it was. Escape in the box empties it and the folders come back.
While the library shows your favourites or your recent documents, the names searched are theirs.

Words are matched in each document's language, so that "invoices" also finds "invoice": the one
the document was read to be written in, or the one the workspace chose on its page
([A document's details](/help/documents)). The passages that hold every word you typed come
first, then those that hold all but one of them, then those that hold any.

- **Filter**, above the passages, narrows them to the documents of some types, dated within a
  span or in a folder. Each part shows beside it, and its cross takes it off. It
  narrows the passages alone, not the files found by their names.
- **Search within these** keeps the words you searched for and empties the box: the next words
  search only the documents with a passage that holds every one of them. Do it again to narrow
  further, up to $within times.

## Searching every project

**Search all projects**, the box on the [projects page](/), searches every project you see at
once when you press Enter, as does **Search every project** under a project's search that found
nothing. Its page lists the best passages first wherever they are, each with its project's name;
click one to open its document at that passage. **Filter** there narrows the search to some of
the projects, to documents of some types or to those dated within a span. A private project is
searched only by those who see it: its chosen members, and the workspace's owners and admins.

## Asking

The chat beside the library answers questions about the project's documents. Emudoc's assistant
looks for what it needs itself: it lists the documents, reads their outlines and summaries,
searches them and reads the passages it found, and it can compare the facts read from several
documents, such as an invoice's prices against the price list. Every claim in an answer cites
its passage with a numbered chip: click it to open the document at that passage and check it.
Answers are written by an AI model and can be wrong; the citations are there so that you can
tell. A document is read for what it says, never obeyed: where one holds instructions addressed
to an AI and they matter to your question, the answer says so.

Stop ends an answer early, and an answer goes on while you look elsewhere, or after you reload
the page. Try again, under the last answer of your chat when it was stopped or failed, writes it
anew in its place. Regenerate, under the last answer once it is finished, writes it again from
the same question, and Edit the question, beside your last question, lets you change it and have
it answered again in its place. A question can be up to $question_chars characters; for more,
upload the text as a document and ask about it. New chat starts afresh.

The chat's title, at the top of the chat, opens the list of your chats. Pin chat, beside a chat
there, keeps it at the top under "Pinned", one you started or one shared with the project; your
pins are yours alone, and Unpin chat puts it back among the others. Under an answer, a line says
which AI model wrote it and how many calls and tokens it took, and to the workspace's owners and
admins what it cost.

## What an answer writes for you

Ask for a reply email, a dispute letter, a summary memo, a comparison table or a report, a Word
document or a memo to keep, an Excel workbook, or for changes to one of your files, and the
assistant writes it into your My files, where only you see it, and the answer's step opens it
([Working together](/help/working-together)).

## Keyboard shortcuts

In a project, Ctrl+Shift+O (⌘+Shift+O on a Mac) starts a new chat, Shift+Esc goes to the box you
ask in, and Esc there stops the answer being written. Ctrl+/ (⌘+/) lists them. They work while
you type in a field too, and wait while a dialog or a menu is open.

## Asking about part of the project

About, above the box you ask in, says what the chat is about and changes it: all the documents,
a folder with its subfolders, or a document open in a tab. The assistant then reads only those,
whatever the question, and tells you when you ask about anything else. The library starts such a
chat from a folder's or a document's menu: Ask about this folder; Summarise this folder, which
asks for its summary at once; and Ask about this document. Choose several files by their boxes,
or with Ctrl (or ⌘) and a click, for Ask about these documents, or two of them for Compare the
two documents. A chat whose folder or documents were deleted reads nothing until you choose
again what it is about.

## Who sees a chat

A chat is yours alone until you share it with the project: Share in the chat's header lets
everyone who sees the project read it, under "Shared with the project", and only you ask in it.
Make private takes it back. A chat is kept until it is deleted, unless the workspace's owners
and admins chose on its page to keep chats a number of days after their last question; a deleted
chat goes into your own trash, under "Deleted chats".

Whoever reads a chat can keep it outside Emudoc: Download the answer, under an answer, saves it
with its question as a Word document, a PDF or Markdown, and the download button in the chat's
header saves every question and answer of the chat in one file. Each citation becomes a numbered
note naming the document, section and pages of its passage. Share the answer, under an answer,
makes a link that shows it to someone without an account ([Working together](/help/working-together)).

## When the assistant cannot answer

The chat says when the AI model is out of reach, and asking is off until it is back; a search
that cannot find passages by their meaning says so, and matches words alone.
